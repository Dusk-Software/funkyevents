Wedding timelines are often treated like a checklist with a clock attached. But a good one does something gentler: it makes space for the parts of the day you want to remember.

## Start with the feeling

Before deciding when hair and makeup begin, think about how you want the morning to feel. Slow and quiet? Full of friends and music? Your answer will shape the pace of everything that follows.

## Build around the moments that matter

Start with the moments that cannot move—your ceremony, venue access, and any fixed vendor arrivals. Then work backward and forward, leaving a little breathing room between the big pieces.

Consider:

- A few extra minutes to get dressed and gather everyone.
- A pause after the ceremony before moving into portraits or dinner.
- Time to say hello to the people you invited.
- A quiet moment together before the evening gets going.

## Leave room for real life

Travel takes time. Guests have questions. A great conversation may last longer than planned. Build in small buffers so the day can bend without feeling behind.

Your planner and vendors can help you make the schedule realistic. The goal isn’t to control every minute; it’s to let the important ones feel unhurried.

## Keep the plan easy to use

Share one clear, current timeline with your vendors and wedding party. Include contact details, locations, and who to ask for help. On the day itself, let your coordinator carry the schedule so you can be where the celebration is.

The best timeline is the one you barely notice—because everything has room to unfold.
